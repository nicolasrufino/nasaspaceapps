HOW WE MAKE THE MAP

Coarse to fine: each stage tells the next where to look

1

Orbit

NASA’s maps of the whole site, already collected. Shows where the science is, where not to go, and where to look closer.

2

Scout

A drone flies the route first and sees the rocks and soft sand orbit can’t. Surprises move from the walk to the planning table.

3

Plan, pack

Route, margin home and load, decided before leaving, because Earth is up to 22 minutes away.

4

Walk, add

The suit display keeps the way home known at every step. Every step adds detail to the map.

5

Next walk

Starts from a better map, so it is safer and goes further.

Orbit: a 5 m map covers 99.5% of Mars; 25 cm images cover about 5%. Scout: Ingenuity flew 72 times and scouted for Perseverance. Plans broke on 1 m maps in NASA’s 2022 JETT3 test.

1:15 to 1:45. Say: we build the map in five stages, coarse to fine. Orbit first, because it's the only data that covers the whole site before anyone lands, and it's free: it shows where the science is and where not to go, and narrows where to look. But orbit can't see a knee-high rock or soft sand, so a scout drone flies the route first, the way Ingenuity scouted for Perseverance. Then we plan the route and the packing list before leaving, because Earth is up to 22 minutes away. During the walk, the suit display keeps the way home known, and every step adds detail. The next walk starts from a better map.
