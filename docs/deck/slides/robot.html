WHY A ROBOT

It gives the crew back time and energy

Lead

Scouts slopes the maps miss. Crews rated “rover leads” acceptable, “crew leads” not.

Carry

Pushing a loaded cart caused unacceptable fatigue in NASA’s field test.

Relay

When comms dropped, an astronaut had to stand still as a relay.

Rescue

If a suit degrades far out, ride home instead of walking.

Today’s Mars rovers average about 110 m/h, 20–30 times slower than a walking astronaut. Ours is specified like NASA’s new Lunar Terrain Vehicle: km/h, with or without crew.

Sources: NASA D-RATS 2022 report; NASA JETT3 report; NASA DRA 5.0; JPL Mars 2020 mobility; NASA LTV award, May 2026.

Use if a judge asks why the rover, or swap in for part of the impact slide. Robots take the slow, heavy, risky work so crew hours go to science.
